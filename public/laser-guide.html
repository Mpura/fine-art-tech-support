Fine Art Tech Support — Rhodes University Fine Art Department · SA Argus 80W Laser

Read this guide before your session. Correct file setup avoids wasted time and re-cuts. Share your file link in your booking request so Tech Support can check it before you arrive.

Software used on this machine

This laser uses a two-program workflow. You prepare your design in CorelDRAW, then send it to the laser through VectorDRAW.

🎨
CorelDRAW
Design & layout
⚡
VectorDRAW
Laser control & path settings

Both programs are installed on the lab computer connected to the laser. You do not need to install anything yourself — just bring your file on a USB stick (under 1 GB, formatted as FAT).

Step 1 — Set up your CorelDRAW file

1
Set the page size
Set your CorelDRAW page/artboard to match the size of your material or the laser bed. The laser bed is 1000mm × 600mm — keep your design within the bounds of your material and leave a small margin from all edges.
2
Use different colours for different operations
Each colour in your file becomes a separate path in VectorDRAW, and each path gets its own speed and power template. Use one colour for cutting lines, a different colour for engraving areas. Keep them on separate layers so they are easy to select.
3
Convert text to curves
Before exporting, select all text and press Ctrl+Q (Arrange → Convert to Curves). This prevents font substitution errors when the file opens on the lab computer.
4
Check your file in Wireframe view
Go to View → Wireframe. What you see in Wireframe is exactly what the laser will cut or engrave. Hidden fills, duplicate lines, or dotted strokes may not behave as expected — clean these up before exporting.
5
Export as .AI (Adobe Illustrator format)
Go to File → Export (Ctrl+E). Choose .AI format. Tick "Export text as Curves" in the export dialog. This is the file you bring to your session — VectorDRAW imports .AI files for vector cutting and engraving.
💡 For photo or image engraving (not vector lines), export as .BMP instead — Grayscale, 150–300 DPI.

Step 2 — Colour coding your paths

VectorDRAW reads each colour in your design as a separate operation. You assign a speed and power template to each colour when you set up the paths in VectorDRAW. There is no single fixed colour standard — but it helps to be consistent. A common setup is:

Suggested colourOperationTypical speed
RedVector Cut — cuts all the way throughSlow (20–60 mm/s)
Blue or otherVector Engrave — scores the surfaceMedium (60–200 mm/s)
Black fillRaster/Sweep Etch — filled areas, imagesFast (200–400 mm/s)
💡 Always engrave (sweep) first, then cut. In VectorDRAW, set engrave paths above cut paths in the path list — the laser processes top-to-bottom.
⚠️ Raster/sweep etching is much slower than vector cutting. A job with heavy raster etching can take significantly longer — plan your session time accordingly.

Step 3 — Know your material

This machine is an 80W CO₂ laser. Ask Tech Support for the correct speed and power settings for your specific material — settings vary and incorrect settings can damage the machine or your work.

Be aware of material warping — wood, card and some plastics warp under heat, which can cause inaccurate cuts. Cutting smaller sheets produces better results.

🚫 Banned materials — DO NOT BRING

These materials produce toxic fumes, damage the machine, or create fire hazards. Any attempt to cut banned materials will result in immediate loss of laser privileges.
  • PVC / vinyl / any chlorine-based material
  • Polycarbonate (Lexan)
  • Fibreglass / coated carbon fibre
  • Foam core / polystyrene foam (not high density foam)
  • Galvanised or mirrored metal
  • HDPE (milk bottle plastic)
  • Pressure-treated wood
  • Casting resins
  • Felt

Not sure about your material? Ask Tech Support before your session — do not bring untested materials on the day.

Step 4 — Securing lightweight materials

Lightweight materials (paper, card, leather, film) can be lifted by the exhaust system, causing incorrect cuts or fire. You MUST secure them before cutting.

Options:

  • Tape the edges to the rulers on the laser bed (bring your own tape)
  • Glue to a heavier board — good for leather and thin plastic
  • Design tabs into your vector file so cut pieces stay connected until you remove them
All jobs must be watched for the full duration of the session. Do not leave the machine unattended.

Step 5 — What happens in VectorDRAW (for reference)

Tech Support will set up and run VectorDRAW. This is just so you understand the process:

1
Import your .AI file
VectorDRAW imports your exported .AI file. Each colour appears as a separate entry in the path list.
2
Assign a template to each colour
Each colour is assigned a speed/power template (e.g. Cut template or Sweep/Engrave template). Engrave paths are placed above cut paths so they run first.
3
Send to machine
The job is sent via USB to the laser's hand control. The machine is zeroed on your material and the job runs.
💡 File names sent to the laser are limited to 8 characters. Tech Support will handle naming — just make sure your file is clearly labelled on your USB.

Step 6 — Share your file before your session

Upload your finished file to Google Drive or WeTransfer and paste the link into your booking request. This lets Tech Support check your file before you arrive — saving time and avoiding re-cuts on the day.

Accepted formats: .AI (vector jobs) or .BMP (photo/image engraving) — not JPEG, PNG or PDF.

💡 If this is your first time using the laser, tick the "First time" box in your request. A short test cut will be done before your main job to confirm settings for your material.

Quick checklist before you submit

  • File exported from CorelDRAW as .AI (or .BMP for photo engraving)
  • All text converted to curves (Ctrl+Q) before exporting
  • Cutting and engraving paths use different colours
  • File checked in Wireframe view — no unwanted lines or hidden objects
  • Material is on the approved list
  • USB stick is under 1 GB and formatted as FAT
  • File link shared in your booking request
  • You will be present for the full session
